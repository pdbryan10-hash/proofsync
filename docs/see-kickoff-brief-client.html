Joining Joblogic to Concerto — what we want out of the kick-off

SEE · ProofSync Kick-off · 75 minutes proof-works.co.uk

Both systems will be worked the way your coordinator works them — signed in as a licensed user of yours, through the screens. So what we can build is decided by what is actually on those screens, and none of that is written down anywhere.

The most valuable thing we can get from this session is not a discussion. It is a recording of one real completion being re-keyed by hand, end to end, together with a capture of the Concerto completion form.

The recording shows us the path. Only the capture gives us the field identifiers, the dropdown values and the validation messages needed to drive it. Between them they take about twenty minutes of the call, and they are what turns this from an estimate into a build.

00

Before the call — please have ready

  • The coordinator who does this every day, at their own desk, able to share screen
  • One completed Joblogic job not yet entered into Concerto — a real one
  • Both logins to hand, and agreement that we may record the screen share
  • Someone who can say who authorises a user account on your domain, and who holds MFA
A

Watch one job cross

Once at normal speed with no commentary, then again slowly with questions. Four things we are watching for, and one we are timing.

The matching key

Which Joblogic field carries the Concerto job reference — a custom field, the PO number, the customer reference? Where does the coordinator read it on screen? Everything hangs off this one item: no reference means no match, and the job becomes an exception rather than a guess. We also want to know what a job with no reference looks like, how often that happens, and whether a reference has ever matched more than one Concerto job.

The completion form, field by field

  • Which fields are mandatory, and what validation fires when they are wrong — we need the exact wording
  • Formats for dates, durations, time on site and costs
  • Which dropdown values the desk actually uses, day to day
  • Which fields we must never touch

Documents

Which certificates and reports go across, where they are fetched from in Joblogic, and what the upload control in Concerto accepts — types, size limits, one at a time or several.

The read-back

What does the coordinator look at to be satisfied it landed? That view is what we compare against. A write is not done until it has been read back — and that rule does not relax because we are driving a screen rather than an interface built for machines.

Time it

Start a timer when they open the completed job, stop it when Concerto is correct. We would much rather publish your measured number than the industry's ten-to-twenty minutes. That figure, against completions per day, is the case — in your numbers rather than ours.

B

Access, and whose name it is in

The operator works as one of your users. It must be visible in your own directory and revocable by you.

  • An email account on SEE's domain for the ProofSync operator
  • A named user login to Joblogic, and a named user login to Concerto — the access your coordinator already holds, not a new grant we ask your client for
  • MFA method confirmed on both, and who holds the second factor
  • Written confirmation from SEE that using an account this way is authorised, and by whom
  • Read-only to start. Where a role without write permission exists, we use it; where it does not, writes stay switched off in configuration and that is verified before credentials are loaded

One test worth two minutes on the call: sign the same Concerto login in twice, from two machines. Does the second session evict the first, or lock the record? If it does, the operator needs its own account rather than sharing your coordinator's — better decided now than discovered mid-run.

C

Scope for phase one

  • Which client contract, and which of your sites
  • Which job types are in, and which are deliberately out
  • How many completions a day, and in what hours
  • Anything seasonal or contractual that moves the volume

E

What we will not do

Nothing is written to prove that writing works. The route is proven offline against the map. Live writes exist only to do real work.
No test record is created in your live system, and we will not ask you for one.
MFA is never bypassed. A person signs in each day and starts the run; the operator rides that session. We never hold a password.
No reference, or an ambiguous match, raises an exception. It never guesses.
Every write is read back and compared before it counts as done.
F

What you leave with

  • The screen and field map for both systems — the thing that makes the build possible, and yours to keep
  • A measured re-keying time and daily volume, so the case is yours rather than ours
  • The phase plan with dates: read-only proof, then offline build, then the first attended write

After the call

We build against the map, not against your systems. The whole journey runs offline first — finding the job, reading the fields, filling the completion form, handling validation, uploading the documents, reading it back — including the paths that fail: no match, ambiguous match, rejection, session expiry. You see that run and agree it is right before anything points at live.

Then the first live runs are on real jobs, with a person present, and each write approved before it is submitted. Per-write approval relaxes only once you have watched enough of them land correctly and tell us so. The attended daily sign-in does not relax at all.
